# Causal Inference Specification

Status: PR 11 spec-only. This document defines causal questions and assumptions before any estimator, report script, API field, or UI surface is implemented.

## Purpose and Boundary

Longevity Lab is primarily a predictive risk communication tool. Predictive models estimate risk patterns from observed covariates. Causal analyses answer different questions: what outcome prevalence would change under a hypothetical intervention, in a target population, under explicit identification assumptions.

These two surfaces must remain separate:

- Predictive risk scores estimate `P(outcome | observed profile)` and may use variables that improve calibrated prediction.
- Causal estimates target potential-outcome contrasts such as `E[Y(1) - Y(0)]` for a named treatment, outcome, and population.
- Predictive feature importance, rule paths, or SHAP values are not causal effects.
- Causal adjustment sets exclude mediators, colliders, leakage variables, and model-derived predictions even when those fields help prediction.
- Causal results must be generated by a separate workbench report and must not silently change Explorer scenario-risk scores.

The initial causal workbench is limited to public BRFSS-derived respondent data plus optional ACS/SVI/EPA context once those feature contracts exist. BRFSS is cross-sectional and self-reported, so these questions are feasibility specifications for assumption-bound estimates of diagnosed-condition prevalence, not clinical incidence claims.

## Shared Design Rules

All causal analyses must follow these rules before estimation:

- Define the population, treatment, outcome, estimand, adjustment set, exclusion rules, and analysis year range.
- Version a DAG assumption block before choosing an estimator.
- Use one persisted question configuration from `conf/causal/questions.yaml`.
- Keep treatment coding reproducible and avoid data-dependent threshold selection.
- Assess overlap/positivity before reporting any effect estimate.
- Report diagnostics, negative controls, sensitivity checks, and limitations next to the estimate.
- Label outputs as exploratory causal estimates under assumptions, never as diagnosis, medical advice, or personalized treatment recommendations.

## Common Candidate Confounders

Candidate confounders are variables plausibly causing both the treatment and the outcome. Final adjustment sets must be question-specific and must pass missingness, overlap, and leakage review.

Common individual-level candidates:

- Age or age band.
- Sex.
- Race and ethnicity.
- Education.
- Income or income band.
- Employment status.
- Marital status.
- Health insurance or healthcare access.
- Usual care or checkup recency when used only as a diagnosis-access proxy.
- State, survey year, and interview season if available.
- Other baseline health behaviors when they are not mediators for the treatment question.

Common contextual candidates, once available:

- ACS poverty, income, education, insurance, disability, broadband, and housing context.
- CDC/ATSDR SVI overall and theme-level context.
- EPA AirData pollutant summaries and completeness flags.
- Census region, state, and urbanicity when available.

Common excluded variable classes:

- The outcome label and any direct components of the outcome label.
- Model predictions, risk deltas, explanation values, or artifact metadata.
- Variables measured after treatment or plausibly downstream of treatment.
- Mediators when estimating a total effect.
- Colliders such as variables conditioned on because of treatment and outcome.
- Quality-of-life or symptom fields that may be consequences of the outcome.
- Fields with ambiguous temporal order unless they are used only in a declared sensitivity analysis.

## Question Registry

The machine-readable registry lives at `conf/causal/questions.yaml`. It contains four spec-only questions:

1. Current smoking and diagnosed chronic lung disease.
2. Meeting physical-activity guidance and diagnosed diabetes.
3. Obesity-range BMI and diagnosed diabetes.
4. Heavy alcohol use and diagnosed depression.

Each question below mirrors the YAML registry and provides the review rationale.

## Question 1: Current Smoking and Chronic Lung Disease

Population:

- Adult BRFSS respondents in supported survey years with valid smoking status, chronic lung disease label, age, sex, geography, and survey weight fields when available.
- Exclude respondents with missing treatment or outcome, impossible age values, proxy records that violate BRFSS analytic guidance if present, and records outside the documented BRFSS adult sampling frame.

Treatment:

- Binary contrast: current smoker versus not current smoker.
- Current smoker should be derived from BRFSS smoking fields using a documented ever-smoked and current-frequency mapping.
- Former smokers stay in the not-current contrast for the primary question only if the estimand is explicitly "current smoking"; a never-smoker contrast is a required sensitivity check.

Outcome:

- Diagnosed chronic lung disease from the modeled BRFSS label contract.
- Treat as prevalent diagnosed disease, not incident disease.

Estimand:

- Primary target: average treatment effect on the risk-difference scale among the eligible BRFSS adult population represented by survey weights.
- Secondary scales: risk ratio and odds ratio for comparison only.

Candidate confounders:

- Age, sex, race/ethnicity, education, income, employment, marital status, insurance, healthcare access, state, year, region, urbanicity if available, ACS/SVI context, EPA pollutant context, physical activity, BMI category, and alcohol-use category.

Excluded variables:

- Chronic lung disease components, respiratory symptoms, poor physical-health days, healthcare utilization plausibly caused by lung disease, model risk outputs, and any post-treatment cessation or medication fields.

DAG assumptions:

- Demographics and socioeconomic context affect smoking and chronic lung disease.
- Healthcare access affects diagnosis probability and may affect smoking cessation.
- Air quality and occupational or environmental context affect chronic lung disease and may correlate with smoking through geography and socioeconomic status.
- Physical activity, BMI, and alcohol use may share upstream causes with smoking and lung disease.
- Unmeasured occupational exposure, early-life exposure, genetics, and secondhand smoke may remain residual confounders.

Negative controls:

- Negative-control outcomes: invariant or pre-treatment fields such as sex-at-birth, race/ethnicity, height, or interview month when present and appropriate for falsification diagnostics.
- Negative-control exposure: within-stratum permuted smoking status to verify the estimator does not produce systematic effects under a placebo exposure.

Sensitivity checks:

- Never-smoker versus current-smoker contrast.
- Exclude former smokers from the reference group.
- Restrict to age ranges with adequate overlap.
- Compare unweighted, survey-weighted, and overlap-weighted estimates.
- E-value or Rosenbaum-style sensitivity for unmeasured confounding.
- DoWhy placebo, subset, and random common-cause refutations in the PR 12 workbench.

Predictive separation:

- The Explorer may use smoking as a predictor of lung-disease risk. That scenario delta is a model response to an input change, not the causal effect of smoking. The causal estimate must be reported separately with its adjustment set, diagnostics, and assumptions.

## Question 2: Physical Activity and Diabetes

Population:

- Adult BRFSS respondents in supported survey years with valid physical-activity fields, diabetes label, core demographics, geography, and survey weights when available.
- Exclude records with missing treatment or outcome and records with pregnancy-related diabetes categories when the label contract supports that exclusion.

Treatment:

- Binary contrast: meets aerobic physical-activity guidance versus does not meet guidance.
- Use documented BRFSS activity minutes or activity-status fields. Do not tune the threshold to maximize observed diabetes separation.

Outcome:

- Diagnosed diabetes from the modeled BRFSS label contract.
- Treat as prevalent diagnosed diabetes, not incident diabetes.

Estimand:

- Primary target: average treatment effect of meeting guidance on diagnosed-diabetes prevalence among eligible BRFSS adults.
- Primary scale: risk difference. Secondary scales: risk ratio and odds ratio.

Candidate confounders:

- Age, sex, race/ethnicity, education, income, employment, marital status, insurance, healthcare access, smoking, alcohol use, state, year, region, urbanicity if available, ACS/SVI context, and EPA pollutant context.
- BMI is not in the primary total-effect adjustment set because it may mediate part of the physical-activity effect. Include BMI only in a declared controlled-direct-effect or robustness analysis.

Excluded variables:

- Diabetes label components, general health, poor physical-health days, activity-limitation fields, medication fields, BMI in the primary total-effect model, model risk outputs, and any variable plausibly caused by diabetes diagnosis.

DAG assumptions:

- Demographics and socioeconomic context affect activity opportunities and diabetes risk.
- Built environment and contextual vulnerability affect activity and diabetes through access, stress, and resource pathways.
- Smoking and alcohol use share upstream causes with activity and diabetes.
- BMI may lie on the pathway from activity to diabetes and may also reflect prior diet and health history, so its role must be handled by sensitivity analysis.
- Reverse causation is plausible because diabetes can reduce activity; analyses must state this limitation.

Negative controls:

- Negative-control outcomes: invariant fields such as sex-at-birth, race/ethnicity, height, or interview month when present.
- Negative-control exposure: within-stratum permuted activity status and an alternate threshold known not to represent guidance as a placebo threshold.

Sensitivity checks:

- Add BMI to estimate a controlled-direct-effect-style contrast and compare with the total-effect estimate.
- Exclude respondents reporting activity limitations if available.
- Trim or overlap-weight poor-propensity-overlap regions.
- Stratify by age band and sex for effect-heterogeneity feasibility.
- Check robustness across survey years and state fixed effects.
- DoWhy placebo, subset, and random common-cause refutations in the PR 12 workbench.

Predictive separation:

- A predictive model can use physical activity and BMI jointly to improve diabetes-risk calibration. A causal total-effect estimate for activity cannot condition on downstream BMI without changing the estimand, so it must be separate from Explorer prediction logic.

## Question 3: BMI and Diabetes

Population:

- Adult BRFSS respondents in supported survey years with valid height, weight, computed BMI, diabetes label, core demographics, geography, and survey weights when available.
- Exclude implausible height/weight values, missing treatment or outcome, underweight respondents from the primary obesity-versus-normal contrast, and pregnant respondents when pregnancy status is available.

Treatment:

- Binary contrast: obesity-range BMI (`BMI >= 30`) versus normal-range BMI (`18.5 <= BMI < 25`).
- Secondary dose-response analyses may compare BMI categories but must be separately declared.

Outcome:

- Diagnosed diabetes from the modeled BRFSS label contract.
- Treat as prevalent diagnosed diabetes, not incident diabetes.

Estimand:

- Primary target: average treatment effect of obesity-range BMI versus normal-range BMI on diagnosed-diabetes prevalence among eligible BRFSS adults represented by the analytic sample.
- Primary scale: risk difference. Secondary scales: risk ratio and odds ratio.

Candidate confounders:

- Age, sex, race/ethnicity, education, income, employment, marital status, insurance, healthcare access, smoking, alcohol use, physical activity, state, year, region, urbanicity if available, ACS/SVI context, and EPA pollutant context.

Excluded variables:

- Diabetes label components, medication fields, hypertension or cardiovascular disease when treated as possible mediators, poor physical-health days, general health, weight-loss attempts after diagnosis, model risk outputs, and variables likely caused by obesity or diabetes.

DAG assumptions:

- Demographics, socioeconomic context, food and activity environment, smoking, alcohol use, and physical activity affect BMI and diabetes risk.
- Healthcare access affects diabetes diagnosis and may correlate with BMI through access to prevention and treatment.
- Some comorbidities may mediate BMI effects and should not be adjusted for in the primary total-effect model.
- Reverse causation is plausible because diabetes diagnosis or treatment can change weight.
- Unmeasured diet, early-life factors, genetics, and medication history may remain residual confounders.

Negative controls:

- Negative-control outcomes: invariant fields such as sex-at-birth, race/ethnicity, or interview month. Do not use adult height as a BMI negative-control outcome because height is part of the treatment definition.
- Negative-control exposure: within-stratum permuted obesity indicator.

Sensitivity checks:

- Compare obesity class I only versus normal BMI to reduce extreme-weight heterogeneity.
- Include overweight as a separate category rather than dropping it.
- Exclude respondents with known diabetes before a documented weight change if timing fields become available.
- Test alternate BMI cutpoints and winsorization of self-reported height/weight.
- Trim or overlap-weight weak-overlap regions.
- E-value or Rosenbaum-style sensitivity for unmeasured confounding.

Predictive separation:

- BMI is an important predictive input for diabetes risk, but a causal BMI question requires a different reference population, exclusion rules, and mediator decisions than the prediction model. Explorer BMI scenario deltas must not be described as the causal effect of changing BMI.

## Question 4: Heavy Alcohol Use and Depression

Population:

- Adult BRFSS respondents in supported survey years with valid alcohol-use fields, diagnosed-depression label, core demographics, geography, and survey weights when available.
- Exclude respondents with missing treatment or outcome. If pregnancy status, current treatment, or abstainer-history fields are available, define their handling before estimation.

Treatment:

- Binary contrast: heavy alcohol use versus not heavy alcohol use.
- Heavy alcohol use should follow a documented BRFSS or CDC threshold using sex-specific daily or weekly consumption fields when available. Binge drinking can be a secondary treatment, not a silent substitute.

Outcome:

- Diagnosed depressive disorder from the modeled BRFSS depression label contract.
- Treat as prevalent diagnosed depression, not incident depression.

Estimand:

- Primary target: average treatment effect of heavy alcohol use on diagnosed-depression prevalence among eligible BRFSS adults.
- Primary scale: risk difference. Secondary scales: risk ratio and odds ratio.

Candidate confounders:

- Age, sex, race/ethnicity, education, income, employment, marital status, insurance, healthcare access, smoking, physical activity, BMI category, state, year, region, urbanicity if available, ACS/SVI context, and EPA pollutant context.

Excluded variables:

- Depression label components, poor mental-health days, current mental-health treatment, general health, social-support proxies measured after exposure, model risk outputs, and variables plausibly downstream of alcohol use or depression.

DAG assumptions:

- Demographics and socioeconomic context affect alcohol use and depression diagnosis.
- Healthcare access affects diagnosis probability and treatment contact.
- Smoking, physical activity, and BMI share upstream causes with alcohol use and depression.
- Depression may influence alcohol use, creating serious reverse-causation risk in cross-sectional data.
- Unmeasured trauma, family history, medication use, and local alcohol policy may remain residual confounders.

Negative controls:

- Negative-control outcomes: invariant fields such as sex-at-birth, race/ethnicity, height, or interview month when present.
- Negative-control exposure: within-stratum permuted heavy-drinking indicator and a placebo threshold that is not a public-health heavy-use threshold.

Sensitivity checks:

- Separate heavy drinking from binge drinking.
- Compare heavy drinkers with moderate drinkers and with all non-heavy drinkers.
- Exclude lifetime abstainers or former drinkers if abstainer-history fields are available to reduce sick-quitter bias.
- Stratify by sex because treatment thresholds and social patterns differ.
- Trim or overlap-weight poor-overlap regions.
- DoWhy placebo, subset, and random common-cause refutations in the PR 12 workbench.

Predictive separation:

- Alcohol use may help predict diagnosed depression in the Explorer. Because depression can also change alcohol use, the causal workbench must report a separate assumption-bound estimate and cannot reuse predictive scenario deltas as causal evidence.

## PR 12 Readiness Criteria

Before implementing estimators, PR 12 should:

- Select one question from `conf/causal/questions.yaml`.
- Materialize the DAG as a versioned graph artifact or text graph in the causal report output.
- Build an analysis dataset with treatment, outcome, weights, selected confounders, and exclusion counts.
- Produce overlap diagnostics before fitting causal estimators.
- Run at least one doubly robust or double machine learning estimator only after diagnostics pass.
- Run the named negative controls and sensitivity checks.
- Write a report that keeps causal estimates out of the API and Explorer UI.
